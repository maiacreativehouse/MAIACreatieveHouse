Before You Begin

Speed isn't the enemy of trust. Denial is. This guide exists to catch the difference before your customers do.

Part 1: The Honesty Check

What's Actually Wrong

There's a real difference between launching something unfinished and launching something you already know is broken. The first is iteration. The second is a decision you're hoping no one notices.

01
Is there a problem I've already identified and I'm hoping no one else notices?

Why This Matters

If you can name the flaw precisely, you already know it's not a "we'll see." It's a "we know." Those get remembered differently by the people who find them.

The Placeholder Test

"Placeholder pricing" and "placeholder messaging" are often just launch anxiety wearing a work-in-progress label. The test is simple: would you be comfortable if a real customer paid, or read, exactly what's live right now?

02
If someone acted on this today, bought at this price, believed this message, would I stand behind it?

One Real Person

Internal feedback is a mirror. Real users are a window. If your messaging has only ever been read by you and people who already understand your business, it hasn't been tested. It's been agreed with.

03
Has one person outside my own head, a stranger, not a friend who already gets it, read this and told me what they actually understood?

Part 2: The Experience Check

If 100 People Showed Up

Most launches are built for a trickle and hoped for a flood. Trust breaks fast when the experience can't hold the attention you were actually asking for.

04
If 100 strangers landed on this today, at once, would the experience hold, or would it visibly buckle?

The "Fix It Later" List

Write down every "we'll fix that after launch" you've said this week. Some of them are genuinely fine to defer. Some of them are the actual product, deferred.

05
Of everything I've deferred to "after launch," which item is actually load-bearing, something the whole experience depends on?
Part 2 · The Experience Check
Your Answer
A Useful Filter

Ask whether the deferred item affects whether someone can get value today, or only how polished that value feels. The first stays on the list. The second can wait.

Part 2: The Experience Check

Every Claim, Checked

Every stat, testimonial, and promise on your launch page is a small trust transaction. Each one either builds credibility or spends it. There's no neutral claim.

06
Does every claim on my page, every number, every "as seen in," every promise, hold up if someone checks it?

Your Answer

It's built by what a stranger concludes on their own.

Part 3: The Trust Check

The Cold Read

You know your own offer so well that clarity stops feeling like a requirement. A cold reader doesn't have that context, and won't ask for it before they leave.

07
If a stranger read this cold, with zero context, do they know what I'm offering and why it matters to them, in the first ten seconds?

The Embarrassment Test

There's usually one thing you're quietly hoping a customer doesn't discover in their first week. Naming it is the fastest way to decide whether it's a real risk or just launch nerves.

08
What's the one thing I'd be embarrassed for a customer to discover in their first week, and have I actually addressed it, or just hoped around it?

Who's Watching Your Back

Momentum makes it easy to skip the loop that actually catches problems: someone honest, looking closely, in the first 48 hours, not a month later in a review no one reads.
